Eyewear Collections

Eyewear for people
who notice everything.

A curated collection of independent designer eyewear from Copenhagen, Tokyo, Paris, Antwerp, Los Angeles, London, Toulouse, and the Veneto. Hand-finished, made-to-measure, built to last.

Curated for People Who Notice

Fourteen houses. Hand-finished, family-run, made to be worn for years.

No conglomerate brands hiding behind fashion-house licensing. No mass-market plastic. Just independent eyewear houses still owned by the people whose name is on the temple — every frame on this page is in our cases right now.

01 Copenhagen · Since 1969

Lindberg

Air-titanium and rimless precision from Denmark. No welds, no screws — every Lindberg is engineered like a piece of architecture, weighing less than a few grams and built to be forgotten on your face. Quiet luxury, made for life.

Discover Lindberg →
02 Paris · Since 1909

Chanel

The house that defines French luxury. Hand-laid acetate, signature CC temple work, and a quiet confidence that doesn't need to shout. Frames designed in Paris, finished by hand — the kind of detail you feel before you see.

Discover Chanel →
03 USA · Made in Italy · Since 2005

Tom Ford

Made in Italy with the discipline of a master tailor. Sculpted lines, signature T-bar metalwork, and an unmistakable proportion. The frame for someone who already owns the suit.

Discover Tom Ford →
04 Los Angeles · Since 1995

Dita

California-designed, Japanese-executed. Gold, titanium, hand-cut acetate — every Dita feels less like eyewear and more like jewelry. The original cult brand that turned independent design into a movement.

Discover Dita →
05 California · Since 2007

Barton Perreira

The polish of vintage Hollywood, made by hand in Japan. Founders Bill Barton and Patty Perreira built the brand on effortless lines and exceptional fit — the frames worn by the people the camera follows.

Discover Barton Perreira →
06 Toulouse · Since 1980

Anne & Valentin

French color theory in eyewear form. Bold, painterly acetate from a husband-and-wife studio in Toulouse who would rather sell you art. Frames as expressive as the people wearing them.

Discover Anne & Valentin →
07 Paris · Since 2006

Thierry Lasry

Parisian futurism. Hand-finished acetate in colors no one else dares, flat-top aviators reimagined for the algorithm age. Worn by every musician you would expect — and most of the ones you wouldn't.

Discover Thierry Lasry →
08 Los Angeles · Since 1979

l.a. Eyeworks

Original LA expression. The frame on the cool kids’ faces for 45 years — Bowie, Patti Smith, Iggy Pop, and now you. Designed by Barbara McReynolds and Gai Gherardi, still independently owned.

Discover l.a. Eyeworks →
09 London · Since 1926

Oliver Goldsmith

British heritage. The frames Audrey Hepburn wore in Breakfast at Tiffany’s, still made by the same family in the same English workshop. Each shape archived; each pair built to outlast trends.

Discover Oliver Goldsmith →
10 Paris · Since 1923

Lafont

A century of Parisian eyewear, still independent and still family-run. Delicate metalwork, hand-laid color, four generations of taste — the most quietly elegant eyewear house on the rue Vignon.

Discover Lafont →
11 Antwerp · Since 1989

Theo

Belgian design that refuses to be normal. Geometric, irreverent, hand-finished in Antwerp — eyewear with the same point of view as the Antwerp Six. For people who feel the same way.

Discover Theo →
12 Veneto · Since 2014

Sospiri

Hand-laid Italian acetate from a small Veneto workshop. Small-batch luxury that pretends to be casual. Soft shapes, deep color, and the unmistakable hand of an Italian atelier.

Discover Sospiri →
13 Copenhagen · Since 1997

Orgreen

Hand-finished Danish titanium in 240 colors and infinite restraint. The kind of frame that looks better the longer you wear it — every Orgreen is sandblasted, anodized, and assembled entirely by hand in Japan.

Discover Orgreen →
14 France · Since 2015

FHone

French acetate cut with generous shapes and color that pops. The newest house on our shelf — already a favorite. Made in small runs, named for the family that makes them.

Discover FHone →
